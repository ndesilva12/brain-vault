# MAKING CINDERELLA — FAN ENGAGEMENT
### Fans don't watch the team. They join it.

**The idea.** Wrexham gave fans a team to love. We give them a **role in it.** Every fan gets a
numbered membership, earns standing by showing up, and can climb to real access — courtside, the team
bus, the film room, a seat next to the GM. **Status is earned, never bought.**

---

## Three ways to participate

| | Who | They give | They get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Member** | Everyone — free | Attention | Numbered ID, votes, open books |
| **Backer** | Thousands | $25–$10,000 | A named thing they funded, plus perks |
| **Owner** | Later, if ever | Real investment | Equity — separate legal track |

## 1 · Membership — free, numbered, permanent

- Free to join. You become **Member #1,847.** Your number never changes.
- **A real vote** on what's genuinely yours: walk-out song · alternate jersey · which charity gets
  game proceeds · the non-conference opponent. *Never lineups — that's coaching.*
- **Open books.** A live public dashboard: roster budget committed vs. spent, sponsors signed,
  tickets sold. **Nobody in college sports shows this.**
- A **"2027 Founding Season"** badge you keep forever.

## 2 · Ranking — you climb by showing up

Points for verifiable action, weighted by what it costs you.

| Action | Points |
|---|---|
| Road game attended | **150** |
| Home game attended | 50 |
| Watch-party check-in | 30 |
| Friend referred who joins | 100 |
| **Your content used in the show** | **500** |
| Vote cast | 10 |
| Merch purchased | 1 per $1 |
| Consecutive games | multiplier |

**Public leaderboard — plus regional boards, city against city.**

## 3 · Rewards — earned, not purchasable

| Tier | How many | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Member | Millions | ID, votes, dashboard |
| Sixth Man | ~100,000 | Early ticket window, store discount |
| Road Crew | ~10,000 | Road-game presale, members' channel |
| Bench | ~1,000 | Courtside lottery, shootaround access |
| Founders Circle | ~100 | Bench seat · team bus · film room · **name on the floor** |
| **GM of the Game** | **1 per game** | **Sit with the GM. Appear in the episode.** |

⭐ **The rule: you cannot buy rank.** If courtside is purchasable, the status is worthless.

## 4 · What fans can buy — products, never positions

Every purchase says exactly what it paid for.

| | |
|---|---|
| **$25** | Founding Member kit — numbered card, sticker, name on the wall |
| **$50** | First-drop tee + name in the credits |
| **$100** | Jersey + name on the floorboard graphic |
| **$500** | ⭐ **You fund the pregame meal for a named road game** — your name on that broadcast |
| **$1,000** | Fund a real item — a film session, an hour on the shooting machine |
| **$2,500** | Two tickets + shootaround + the team bus |
| **$5,000** | **Your name on the court, permanently** |
| **$10,000** | Associate Producer credit + bench seat |

**Merch is the story.** Every drop posts a counter: *"this drop funded $318,000 of the roster."*

**Named micro-sponsorships.** $500 puts a fan's name on one road-trip broadcast — the same product we
sell a brand, sold small.

## 5 · The launch — The Adoption Draft

Before the season, **the internet votes on which school gets the investment.** Four programs already
under letter of intent. Bracket format, run over several weeks.

- **Four fanbases mobilise themselves** — free audience at scale
- **The schools campaign to be chosen**, instead of us persuading them
- **The winner arrives with a mandate** — the emotional core of the whole story
- ⭐ **And it is episode one.**

## 6 · The four rules

1. **Status is earned.** Money buys products, never rank.
2. **Fans vote on culture, never on competition.** Jerseys yes, lineups no.
3. **Every dollar shows what it bought.**
4. **Fans never contact recruits.** Written into the membership terms. No exceptions.

---

### Why it works

**A streamer isn't buying a basketball team. They're buying an audience that already cares.**

50,000 members who chose the school, voted on the jersey and funded the charter flight **are the
asset** — and they exist before episode one airs.
