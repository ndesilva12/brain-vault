# DOCUMENT 08 — Board Composition Memo (the parents / 3-vs-4 question)
_Session 2026-07-22 · recommendation, not legal advice._

## Question
Board of 3 that Norman owns now. Norman may appoint his father and/or mother and
let Ankur come in as 1 of 4. Thoughts?

## Recommendation: **keep the board at 3, not 4.**
Odd number, no deadlock. A 4-person board splits 2–2 and stalls; the tie has to
be broken by a chair casting-vote or a shareholder vote — needless friction you
control your way out of anyway. **3 is the right number and it's already set in
Doc 06.**

## On seating a parent
- **The math still favors you at 3.** Norman + one parent + Ankur = **2 of 3**
  board seats loyal to you. Add the dual-class super-vote and you control the
  *shareholder* election of the whole board regardless — the board seats are
  belt-and-suspenders, not the thing that keeps you in control.
- **A parent is a reliable vote — but it's the weakest seat on optics.** When you
  go to raise the next round, a sophisticated investor sees "founder + founder's
  father" and reads it as a rubber-stamp board, not real governance. It can cost
  you credibility exactly when you want the board to look like adult supervision.
- **Independence is worth more than loyalty here** — because you don't *need* the
  loyalty. The Class B super-vote is your control. Given that, spend the seat on
  someone who makes the company look better to the next check: an experienced
  operator, a respected industry name, or simply leave it **vacant** (you hold
  the vacancy-fill power) until the right independent shows up.

## Practical recommendation
1. **Seat 1: Norman** (locked).
2. **Seat 3: Ankur** at the Aug 15 close (the investor seat he'll expect).
3. **Seat 2: leave vacant for now**, controlled by you.
   - If you want a friendly vote before Aug 15, one parent in Seat 2 is *fine and
     safe* — you keep 2 of 3 — just know you'll likely want to swap it for an
     independent before Series A.
   - **Cap it at one parent, never two.** Two family seats + you = a board that
     looks captured and adds real conflict-of-interest exposure (related-party
     transactions get scrutinized harder).
4. **Ankur as "1 of 4" — skip it.** Don't go to 4 to fit everyone. Keep 3, and
   Ankur takes the one investor seat.

## Bottom line
You already have control from the super-voting shares — so use the board seats to
*look* well-governed, not to stack friendly votes you don't need. 3 seats:
you, Ankur, and an open seat you fill with an independent (or a single parent as
a temporary placeholder). Never 4, never two family members.
