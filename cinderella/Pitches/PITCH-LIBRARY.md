# Making Cinderella — Pitch Library

_Every pitch, description, and explainer we've actually used, organized by **audience** then
**length**. Started 2026-09-03. **Append new versions; don't overwrite old ones** — the point is
to see what language has already been in front of whom._

**How to use this:** find the audience, take the shortest version that fits the channel, adapt.
Every entry carries the date it was used and a note on what it does well, so a future rewrite
starts from evidence instead of from scratch.

---

## A. CELEBRITY / TALENT

The hardest audience — they read three lines and decide. The objection to kill first is
**"how much is this going to cost me."**

### A1. The short pitch ⭐ CURRENT BEST — Norman's tightening, 2026-09-02

> Making Cinderella is Welcome to Wrexham meets D1 men's basketball — rebuilding a small
> program into a national contender overnight.
> • We pair the school with a famous alum or celebrity who becomes the lead investor
> (**no capital required**) and create a docu-series around the journey
> • The revenue committed to the docu-series funds the NIL for the players, to support a
> **top-10 budget nationally**
> • Most importantly, we have agreed to long-term and significant revenue shares with the
> school to economically participate in their success.

**Why it works:** "no capital required" removes the biggest objection inside the first bullet,
before they've decided to stop reading. "Top-10 budget nationally" is a number they can picture;
"build a national contender" was aspiration. "Overnight" carries the whole speed argument in one
word.

**Used for:** the Stephen A. Smith approach (`cinderella.short.gy/sas`), 2026-09-02.

### A2. The prior version — Curry-anchored, use only when Curry is the hook

> Making Cinderella is Welcome to Wrexham meets D1 men's basketball — a scalable sports media
> platform built around rebuilding small programs into national contenders.
> • We pair each school with a famous alumnus and create a docu-series around the journey
> • The revenue from the docu-series funds the NIL for the players to build a national contender
> • Most importantly, we have agreed to long-term and significant revenue shares with the school
> to economically participate in their success.
> For our first season we have already locked in Stephen Curry and Davidson. That gives the
> project instant credibility, cultural relevance, and a strong hook for premium sports
> storytelling.

⚠️ **Do NOT send this to a celebrity you're recruiting.** Leading with Curry/Davidson reads as
*"the seat is taken."* The Curry line is a **credibility** asset for buyers and investors and a
**deterrent** for talent. A2 is the buyer/investor variant of A1, not a talent pitch.

### A3. One-liner

> Welcome to Wrexham meets D1 men's basketball.

Six words, does 80% of the work. Lead with it in a text, a DM, or the first line of a cold email.

## B. BUYERS / STREAMERS

Standing decision (see `Process/2026-08-28-partner-buyer-landscape-and-network-map.md`): **lead
with the show, reveal the franchise second.** Opening with six celebrity names reads unfocused.

### B1. The attachment framing — default

> Season 1 is locked — Curry at Davidson. Behind it: Foxx/North Texas, Sandler/UNH, and others.
> We'd love your input on Seasons 2 and 3.

Preserves buyer agency on the slate without surrendering the anchor. **Do not go in unattached** —
"we could attach a celebrity" is worth roughly nothing.

### B2. The HBO variant — for a buyer who wants an unknown school

> The franchise launches with Curry/Davidson — the school in our slate that fits your brief is
> Chicago State / CSUN.

Same franchise, different entry point. Bentley Weiner's brief is (1) a truly small school that
has never won, (2) a clear POV — *"what does it interrogate?"*

### B3. The full platform one-pager

`Outputs/format-one-pager.md` (and the .docx beside it). Covers concept, why now, format,
what comes with it, why it can't be copied, and the offer. **This is the long-form buyer
document** — a season license, live-event rights, multi-season franchise; Cinderella Corp
retains format and franchise.

### B4. The logline, from that one-pager

> The Cinderella run — engineered. A celebrity, private capital, and an overlooked Division I
> basketball program, transformed into a national phenomenon and captured as a premium
> docuseries. Welcome to Wrexham for American college hoops — but a franchise, not a one-off.

## C. INVESTORS / CAPITAL

### C1. Structural one-sentence

> Cinderella Corp. is the parent IP company behind Making Cinderella — a sports entertainment
> franchise pairing high-profile celebrities with small Division I college basketball programs,
> structured as Wrexham-style documentary investments.

### C2. The model in one breath

> Parent raises $1.5M for 15%. Each school is a 5-year SPV; the parent takes 40% plus a 10%
> franchise fee and a 2% annual management fee. The SPV takes 80% of the school's incremental
> commercial revenue. MOIC rises with every school added, because parent costs are fixed.

⚠️ **Representation discipline** (standing rule): state only what's provable — **signed LOIs from
Davidson and Saint Joseph's, active capital discussions, Curry engaged via Unanimous.** TCG is
*conditional*, not committed. Prior outbound said "funded @ $15mm by TCG" and "raised ~$45M";
do not repeat either.

## D. SCHOOLS / ATHLETIC DIRECTORS

### D1. The honest close — used with Ryan Colton (new UNH AD), 2026-09-01

> Truthfully, this comes down to Adam Sandler. If we can get him, it's yours.

**Why it works:** total candor about the real dependency, and it converts the AD from an
evaluator into a rooting interest. Reusable pattern: *name the one condition, then hand them
the outcome.*

## E. THE STORY VERSION (press, sizzle, narrative)

### E1. "Band of the overlooked" — from the sizzle reel, rev. 2026-08-08

> Big money doesn't just beat small schools — it **buys their best players away**, every year.
> Making Cinderella flips that: the overlooked, the passed-over, the players who'd normally sell
> out to a blue blood one by one instead **team up at a small school and go beat the giants at
> their own game.** The same money the big schools use to *take* them now **keeps them
> together.** And not one Cinderella — **several, in the same season.**

This is the emotional engine, and the only version that answers *"what does it interrogate?"*
Use it for press, for HBO-tier buyers, and anywhere the room needs to feel it rather than
model it.

## Standing rules for any pitch

1. **Kill the cost objection in the first three lines** when talking to talent.
2. **Curry is credibility with buyers, a deterrent with talent.** Never lead with him to a
   celebrity you're recruiting.
3. **Source-verify every celebrity–school tie before it goes in writing** (standing rule after
   the Luke Murray/Loyola error).
4. **Claim only what's provable** — see C2's discipline note.
5. **Never let a partner represent attachment before signature.**
