## DRAFTING NOTE — Title selection (Section 1.1)

_Not part of the Agreement. Delete before execution._

The protective machinery lives in **Recitals A–D** and **Sections 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 1.6 and 1.7** — not in the title. Those sections operate identically whichever title is selected, so the choice below is a **positioning decision, not a legal one.**

| Option | Reads as | Origination risk | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founding Partner, Partnerships** *(drafted default)* | Senior, domain-bounded | Low | The comma caps the claim: partner *of partnerships*, not *of the enterprise* |
| Partner, Partnerships | Senior, no founder adjacency | Lowest | Cleanest; slightly flatter |
| Day 1 Partner *(prior draft)* | Explicitly sequenced | Lowest | Non-standard; implies someone was "Day 0," which he will feel |
| Managing Director, Partnerships | Senior in the sports/entertainment register | Low | Inherently a rank — implies people above and below |
| Executive Vice President, Partnerships | Clearly second tier | Low | Unambiguously not #1 |

**Avoid "Chief ___ Officer" titles.** They carry no authority under the Bylaws, but they read to third parties as *"can sign"* — creating apparent-authority exposure that Section 1.6(g) then has to work to undo.

**Recommended pairing (shared vocabulary makes rank self-evident):**
> Norman — **Founder & Managing Partner** · Partner — **Partner, Partnerships**

Mixed registers ("Founder & CEO" vs. "Founding Partner") leave outsiders unable to rank the two, which is how a #2 gets read as co-equal. A single vocabulary removes the ambiguity without anyone having to explain it.
