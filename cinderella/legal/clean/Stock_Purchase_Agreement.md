# STOCK PURCHASE AGREEMENT
### Cinderella Corp. — Class A Common Stock

This Stock Purchase Agreement (this "**Agreement**") is entered into as of the date set forth on the signature page, by and between **Cinderella Corp.**, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"), and the undersigned purchaser (the "**Investor**").

---

## 1. PURCHASE AND SALE

**1.1 Purchase.** Subject to the terms of this Agreement, the Investor irrevocably subscribes for and agrees to purchase the number of shares of the Company's **Class A Common Stock** (the "**Shares**") set forth on the signature page, at a purchase price of **$1.00 per share**, for the aggregate purchase price set forth on the signature page (the "**Purchase Price**"). The minimum allocation is $100,000, representing 100,000 Shares and 1.0% of the Company on a post-closing basis.

**1.2 Revenue-Share Right.** Concurrently with the purchase of the Shares, and in addition to them, the Company will grant the Investor a Revenue-Share Right pursuant to a Revenue-Share Agreement of even date, entitling the Investor to receive 50% of the Company's Gross Cash Revenue until the Investor has received 1.0× the Purchase Price, and thereafter 10% of Gross Cash Revenue until the Investor has received 2.0× the Purchase Price, after which the right terminates.

**1.3 Acceptance.** This subscription is not binding on the Company until accepted by the Company, which may accept or reject it in whole or in part in its sole discretion.

**1.4 Closing.** The closing will occur in a single closing targeted for **September 15, 2026**, for an aggregate offering of up to $1,500,000 and 1,500,000 Shares. The Company may call an additional $1,500,000 on the same terms following the closing.

**1.5 Payment.** The Investor will pay the Purchase Price by wire transfer in accordance with instructions delivered by the Company prior to closing.

**1.6 Governing Documents.** As a condition to closing, the Investor will execute a counterpart signature page to the Company's **Stockholders' Agreement** and the **Revenue-Share Agreement**, and will hold the Shares subject to the Company's Amended and Restated Certificate of Incorporation and Bylaws.

## EXHIBIT B — RISK FACTORS

An investment in the Company involves a high degree of risk. Prospective investors should carefully consider the following risks, which are not exhaustive.

**Early-stage business; risk of total loss.** The Company is an early-stage enterprise with a limited operating history and no established record of revenue. Investors may lose their entire investment.

**No control over management.** Voting control rests exclusively with the holder of Class B Common Stock. Investors have no board representation and no ability to influence the Company's decisions, strategy, or management.

**Illiquidity.** The Shares are restricted securities. There is no public market for the Shares, none is expected to develop, and transfers are subject to significant restrictions. Investors may be unable to liquidate their investment.

**Speculative projections.** The Company's business model depends on assumptions regarding sponsorship, media licensing, school-level commercial revenue, and franchise expansion, none of which is assured. Actual results may differ materially from any projection furnished.

**Regulatory and NCAA risk.** The Company's business depends on the evolving regulatory environment governing name, image, and likeness rights, revenue sharing, and collegiate athletics generally. Adverse rule changes, litigation, or legislation could materially impair the business model.

**Dependence on key persons and third parties.** The business depends substantially on Norman C. de Silva and on relationships with celebrities, institutions, distribution platforms, sponsors, and capital partners. The loss of key personnel or the failure to secure or maintain these relationships would materially harm the Company.

**Concentration.** Early operations will depend on a small number of schools and celebrity partners. The failure of any one arrangement could have a disproportionate effect.

**Additional capital and dilution.** The Company may issue additional securities, including an employee equity pool, which would dilute existing holders. Investors have no anti-dilution protection and no preemptive rights.

**Limits on the return of capital.** The return-of-capital right is limited to the Company's available, undeployed cash and may not result in the return of all or any portion of an investment.

**Revenue-Share Right is contractual and unsecured.** The Revenue-Share Right is a contractual obligation of the Company, is unsecured, is subordinate to senior indebtedness, and depends entirely on the Company generating Gross Cash Revenue. There is no assurance that any payments will be made.

**Tax matters.** The tax treatment of the Shares and the Revenue-Share Right is uncertain and depends on each investor's circumstances. Investors should consult their own tax advisors.
