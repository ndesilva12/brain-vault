## ⚠️ Open terms that actually matter

**1. Investor economics don't fit the current charter — the #1 item.** Your charter authorizes only **Class A / Class B *Common*, "identical except voting," with equal dividends/distributions.** The promised **revenue-share preference** is a *preferred* right common stock can't carry. Fix with Loeb, choose one:
- **(a) Common + separate Revenue-Share Agreement** (investors get 15% Class A Common **plus** a contractual 50%/10%-to-2× revenue-share right). Clean cap table. **Recommended.**
- **(b) Authorize a Series Seed Preferred** in the charter carrying the revenue-share preference. Requires another charter amendment.
Also: a **% of *gross revenue*** is an unusual equity feature (revenue-based-financing / royalty flavor); Loeb should confirm structure and tax.

**2. Control isolation — RESOLVED by your charter.** Only Norman holds Class B (10×); everyone else (founders + investors) holds Class A (1×); Class B auto-converts on transfer. Just issue accordingly and record it in the stock ledger and Stock Purchase Agreement.

**3. Tranching (capital efficiency → retained equity).** Returning undeployed cash alone (the Ankur right) does **not** claw back equity. To give up less equity when you deploy less, size a **smaller first close with the remainder callable at the same valuation** — issue shares only as capital is called. **[Decide first-close size.]**

**Other flags:** exact per-share price/share counts; founder share counts + vesting; the additional-$1.5M-call mechanics; drag/tag; venue/arbitration; the corp's tax reporting (1099s, not K-1s).
