## Item-by-item

### 1. Investor Representative / D&O — SPLIT: one grant, one decision

**1(a) "Confirm a D&O policy is actually in place, the limits, and that I am covered."**

⚠️ **This is not a drafting item and the answer is already in his own document.**
**Side Letter §5(d)** states expressly: *"The Company represents that, as of the date hereof, it
does not maintain directors' and officers' liability insurance,"* with a commercially-reasonable-
efforts obligation to obtain it within 90 days. **Register 3.15 confirms: no D&O exists.**

So the honest answer to him is "there is no policy, as §5(d) discloses." Whether to buy one is
**Norman's decision** — see Decisions below.

**1(b) "No personal liability to an Investor for good-faith acts as Investor Representative,
except fraud, bad faith, willful misconduct or gross negligence."**

✅ **GRANT.** This is standard exculpation with the standard carve-outs, and the carve-outs he
proposes are already the ones in SHA §2.6 and Adviser §4.2. Indemnification (Company pays his
costs) and exculpation (he has no liability in the first place) are different protections; he
currently has only the first. Costs Norman nothing because the Company already indemnifies him
for exactly this conduct.

**New Stockholders' Agreement §2.7 — insert after §2.6:**

> **2.7 No personal liability of the Investor Representative.** The Investor Representative shall
> have no personal liability to any Investor for any action taken, or omitted to be taken, by him
> in good faith in his capacity as Investor Representative, except to the extent such action or
> omission constitutes fraud, bad faith, willful misconduct or gross negligence. The Investor
> Representative is not a fiduciary of, and owes no fiduciary duty to, any Investor, and may have
> interests that differ from those of any Investor. Each Investor, by executing this Agreement or
> a joinder to it, acknowledges and agrees to this Section 2.7. Nothing in this Section creates
> any liability or duty that would not otherwise exist, and nothing in this Section limits the
> indemnification provided under Section 2.6. This Section 2.7 survives termination of this
> Agreement.

### 2. RSPA cleanup — GRANT, with one finding that changes his answer

**2(a) Purchase-price / FMV internal inconsistency.** ✅ Real, and worse than he says — the RSPA
states it **three different ways**:
- Header note: *"Purchase price per Share to be set at fair market value as determined in good
  faith by the Board — confirm with counsel before execution"*
- §1.1: *"at a purchase price of $0.001 per Share"*
- Schedule A: *"Purchase price per Share: $0.001 [FMV as determined by the Board]"*

**Fix — make all three read the same.** Delete the header note entirely. Replace §1.1 and the
Schedule A line with:

> **1.1 Shares.** The Company issues and sells to Purchaser, and Purchaser purchases, 166,667
> shares of Class A Common Stock, par value $0.001 (the "Shares"), at a purchase price of $0.001
> per Share, which the Board has determined in good faith to be the fair market value of a share
> of Class A Common Stock as of the Effective Date.

> *Schedule A —* **Purchase price per Share: $0.001 (Board-determined fair market value as of the
> Effective Date)**

⚠️ **Dependency: a Board resolution fixing $0.001 as FMV must actually exist and be dated on or
before the Effective Date.** $0.001 is defensible pre-priced-round, but the defense is the
resolution, not the number. If it hasn't been adopted, adopt it before signature.

**2(b) The `[Rule 701 / Regulation D]` placeholder.** ✅ Must be finalized — and ⭐ **my
recommendation is the opposite of the default.**

**Do not use Rule 701.** Rule 701 covers compensatory grants to consultants and advisors, **but
it excludes services rendered in connection with the offer or sale of securities in a
capital-raising transaction.** Ankur's own §1.1 defines his services as *"(a) advice on financial
structure, valuation, and capital formation"* and *"(c) participation in PE/investor and
distributor meetings."* That is capital-raising participation on the face of the document. §1.5's
no-broker language helps but does not cure the 701 carve-out.

**Use Section 4(a)(2) / Regulation D Rule 506(b).** He is accredited, this is a private
placement, and the exemption does not depend on characterizing his services as non-capital-raising.

> **1.3 Securities exemption.** The Shares are issued in a transaction exempt from registration
> under Section 4(a)(2) of the Securities Act of 1933, as amended, and Rule 506(b) of Regulation D
> thereunder. The Shares have not been registered and may not be transferred absent registration
> or an available exemption.

⚠️ **Loeb should confirm.** Flagging the 701 capital-raising carve-out to them explicitly — it is
the reason the obvious answer is the wrong one here.

**2(c) Milestone shares on objective achievement, not a discretionary Company determination.**
✅ **GRANT — he is right.** RSPA §2.4 currently makes issuance contingent on *"the Company's
written determination that the applicable milestone has been achieved,"* which makes an earned
milestone unenforceable at the Company's option. That is not what was agreed.

**The balanced fix keeps Norman's attribution protection while removing the veto.** Replace §2.4:

> **2.4 Milestone grants.** Where Schedule A identifies the Shares as a milestone grant, the
> Shares are issuable upon objective achievement of the applicable milestone as defined in
> Purchaser's separate written agreement with the Company, and are fully vested on issuance.
> Article 3 does not apply to such Shares. Issuance is not subject to any further determination,
> consent or discretion of the Company or the Board. Purchaser shall deliver written notice to the
> Company identifying the milestone achieved and the facts establishing achievement and
> attribution, and the Company shall issue the Shares within fifteen (15) business days following
> that notice. If the Company disputes achievement or attribution, it shall deliver written notice
> of the dispute, stating the grounds in reasonable detail, within ten (10) business days of
> Purchaser's notice; absent such timely notice the milestone is deemed achieved and attributable.
> The milestone definitions, the attribution requirement under Section 2.2(d) of that agreement,
> and the aggregate cap on equity issuable across milestones are governed exclusively by that
> agreement.

**2(d) 83(b).** ✅ **Answer: not required, and not advisable, for the initial 4%.** An 83(b)
election applies only to property that is *substantially nonvested* at transfer. The Role Shares
are **fully vested on the Effective Date**, so there is nothing to elect on — income is recognized
at grant regardless. The same is true of milestone shares, which §2.4 issues fully vested.

Adviser Agreement §2.7 currently says *"Adviser is advised to consider an 83(b) election within
thirty (30) days of each grant,"* which is misleading here. Replace:

> **2.7 Mechanics; tax.** Issued as restricted stock under the Company's Restricted Stock Purchase
> Agreement and subject to the Stockholders' Agreement. The Role Shares and any milestone shares
> are fully vested on issuance and accordingly an election under Section 83(b) of the Internal
> Revenue Code is inapplicable to them; Adviser should consult his own tax advisor as to the
> treatment of each grant.

### 3. Financial materials / his role — ⭐⭐ GRANT IMMEDIATELY. This is a win.

**What he is giving up:** this was his original "Item 2" — the approval right over Company
financial materials — which Norman conceded, and which §§1.2–1.4 were carefully engineered to make
survivable (approval attaching to materials rather than decisions, deemed given on participation
or five business days' silence, DGCL authority recited). **He is now withdrawing the approval
right entirely.** His motive is his own liability exposure, but the effect is that Norman recovers
the single largest control concession in the document.

⚠️ **One guardrail to add that he did not propose:** "consult in good faith" is a process
obligation, and without an express carve-out it can be argued as a condition precedent to a
financing — a veto by another name. The replacement below closes that.

**Replace §1.1(b), §1.2 and §1.3. Keep §1.4 as drafted (he asked to keep CPA selection).**

> **1.1 Role.** Adviser serves as a non-exclusive Strategic Adviser, providing: (a) advice on
> financial structure, valuation, and capital formation; (b) strategic input on the Company's
> financial presentation as set forth in Section 1.2; (c) participation in PE/investor and
> distributor meetings (subject to OBA Approval and Section 1.5); and (d) introductions across his
> network.

> **1.2 Financial presentation — consultation; no approval or review duty.** The Company will
> consult Adviser in good faith on the Company's financial presentation, valuation, financing
> structure and proposed investor terms, and Adviser may provide strategic input as he considers
> appropriate. **Adviser has no duty to review, approve, verify, audit or monitor any financial
> materials, model, assumption, valuation or investor term, and no right of approval over any of
> them.** Responsibility for the preparation, accuracy and completeness of the Company's financial
> materials rests with the Company, its Board of Directors and the accounting firm retained under
> Section 1.4. Adviser's participation in, or receipt of, any materials does not constitute
> approval, verification or endorsement of them, and his silence or non-response does not
> constitute approval.

> **1.3 No condition; no remedy.** The consultation contemplated by Section 1.2 is a covenant of
> the Company only. It is not a condition precedent to any financing, issuance of securities,
> acceptance of any subscription, or any other corporate action, and no failure to consult shall
> invalidate, delay or give rise to any claim in respect of any such action. The issuance of
> securities, the consideration for which they are issued, and the acceptance of any subscription
> remain matters for determination by the Board under the Delaware General Corporation Law, the
> Company's Certificate of Incorporation and its Bylaws.

⭐ **Note the interaction with Item 1:** granting this materially reduces the liability he is
worried about in 1(a). Removing the duty removes most of the exposure. Worth saying to him.

undeployed capital at any time in his sole discretion, not subject to any anniversary or milestone.

**It already does, in both documents:**

  Representative may, in his sole discretion and at any time, require the Company to return

> …the Investor Representative may, in his sole discretion and at any time — without regard to any
> anniversary of the date hereof and without regard to the Company's achievement of any operating,
> financing or other milestone — require the Company to return Undeployed Seed Capital to the
> investors on a pro rata basis in accordance with the Side Letter Agreement…

**The guardrails Norman set stay as drafted and were not reopened:** seed proceeds only, capped at
aggregate Investment Amounts, legally-available limitation, and the anti-starvation covenant in

## Decisions Norman must make

### A. D&O insurance — buy it or disclose it

There is no policy. Three things now turn on it: Ankur's Investor Representative role, his board
seat, and Greg's adviser paper. **CAA Evolution diligence will ask.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | Roughly $5–15k/year for $1–2M of coverage at this stage |
| **Against** | Real money against a ~$650k trimmed budget, for a company with no revenue and no employees |
| **For** | It is already a contractual obligation (Side Letter §5(d), 90 days), it gates two advisers and a board seat, and it is the kind of thing a banker treats as a hygiene signal |

⭐ **Recommendation: bind a small policy before the seed opens, not before signature.** Tell Ankur
plainly that §5(d) already discloses there is no policy, that the exculpation clause in new SHA
§2.7 gives him the protection he is actually asking for, and that the policy follows at the seed
close. That is honest, it costs nothing now, and it does not hold up signature.

### B. Securities exemption — Rule 701 is probably unavailable

As above: his services expressly include capital formation and investor meetings, which runs into
Rule 701's capital-raising carve-out. **Recommendation: Section 4(a)(2) / Rule 506(b), and put the
question to Loeb in writing** so the file shows the analysis.

### C. ⚠️ The capital-return right is a financeability problem — flagging, not reopening

Norman has conceded this twice and I am not reopening it. But he should know the cost with CAA
Evolution in the room: **"one investor may pull all undeployed capital at will, at any time, with
no vote and no milestone" is a structural flag any banker will mark.** It is disclosed as Risk
Factor 13, which is correct, and it is exactly the kind of term that makes an institutional
investor reprice or pass.

⭐ **Two asks that cost Ankur nothing and make the company financeable — recommend making them:**
1. **30 days' written notice** before exercise (§6(b) already gives the Company 30 days to pay;
   notice simply moves it in front).
2. **Sunset on a priced round** — the right terminates on the closing of a subsequent equity
   financing above a stated size, at which point undeployed seed capital is a meaningless concept
   anyway.

Neither touches his sole discretion. Both remove the "can be pulled at any moment" problem for a
future investor.
