# Streamer Go-to-Market Playbook — what Norman is walking into (2026-08-05)
_What to have clean for a Netflix/streamer meeting, the standard process, how the Steph/Unanimous
"locked-in" question actually plays out, and what to expect across Netflix / Amazon / Apple /
Paramount-Skydance._

## 1. The single most important reframe: pitch the FRANCHISE, not a celebrity
We are pitching a **multi-school Season 1**. That structure means **no single celebrity is
load-bearing** — the *format + school slate + operating system* is the product; celebrities are
interchangeable pairings on top of it. This is the answer to almost every Steph worry below, and
it's Norman's strongest position: **be the slate/format/ops owner; let the buyer cast.**

## 2. The Steph / Unanimous question — how it actually plays out
- **Correcting an earlier note:** big talent **won't** sign an LOI pre-deal — Erick is right that
  there's no upside for Steph, and Sunjay confirms it's standard at his stature. So "get Steph on
  paper now" was the wrong goal. **Talent firms up at deal time (real money + greenlight), not
  before.** The unconfirmed verbal is the *normal* state, not a failure.
- **Read on the slow-walk:** Unanimous may be slow because they're exploring a **larger Davidson
  capital play (beyond TCG)** and see Cinderella as one of several sources — i.e., not
  prioritizing us. Treat Steph as a *likely flagship pairing, not a promised deliverable.*
- **If we land Netflix and Unanimous then makes demands:** it becomes a **contained talent
  negotiation**, not a threat to the franchise deal — **provided no single talent is a condition
  of the deal** (see term-sheet logic; structure the format deal and the talent deals separately).
  Netflix resolves it the normal way: pay the quote and meet reasonable terms, **or recast.**
- **Will a difficult Steph put Netflix off / kill it?** **No — if the concept + schools + format
  are strong.** Netflix doesn't buy a franchise for one verbal; they buy the idea and then cast
  it. If anything, a difficult Steph makes them *prefer talent they control.* Their likely
  reaction is exactly what Norman guessed: **"fine — we'll call Hart / Sandler / Chappelle."**
  Because **we already have authentic schools for those names**, that's a *strength*, not a
  scramble. The multi-school slate is the insurance policy.
- **What we DON'T want to lose:** Steph is still the best *proof-of-concept* and most exclusive
  pairing (Davidson's magic is Curry). He's a huge **asset for the pitch** — just not a **single
  point of failure.** Keep him warm; don't build the spine around him.
- **Net rule:** never make any one celebrity a condition precedent to the Cinderella↔buyer
  format deal. Come with a *slate of pairings* so the buyer can cast and no one can hold us up.

8. **Why-us** — the operating system + locked schools + relationships they can't replicate fast.

## 4. Standard Netflix (and streamer) process — what to expect
1. **First meeting = a pitch/temperature meeting, not a negotiation.** 30–60 min, usually with a
   development/unscripted exec (not the top). Goal: get them excited + a second meeting. Do **not**
   walk in expecting to close terms.
2. **Internal championing.** A liking exec carries it up the chain (VP → head of unscripted →
   greenlight). This is why an inside champion matters (Kitty; Jeremy's Netflix friend; the
   Sarandos proximity as air cover).
3. **Follow-up / development.** If interested: more materials, treatment, budget, maybe an option
   or a hold. Unscripted often skips long development and moves to order when there's heat.
4. **Deal negotiation** (business affairs/legal — separate people from the creative exec, and they
   drive hard): rights & ownership (format, exclusivity, term, territory), license fee vs.
   cost-plus, backend, credits (created-by/EP), talent, production entity, **international
   rights**, and **renewal options for future seasons** (protect the franchise here).
5. **Greenlight / order**, then production.
- **Timelines:** fast with heat (weeks–months to a yes), slow or silent without it. Netflix moves
  fast when they want something and goes dark when they don't.
- **Watch-outs:** they'll want to **own IP + control casting** (hold the format + created-by, give
  them the season); they may ask for an **exclusive hold** while deciding (negotiate a short
  window); BA will push on everything — the creative "yes" is not the deal.

## 5. Across the buyers — what to expect where
- **Netflix** — biggest reach/prestige; data-driven on talent following; **now heavily into live
  sports** (NFL, WWE) → the live-games hook lands hard; aggressive on owning IP + global rights;
  moves fast when hot. (Ankur→Sarandos; Jeremy's friend; Sunjay→Gaspin.)
- **Amazon (Prime/MGM)** — **has NBA rights (2025-26+)** → arguably the *most natural* fit for a
  basketball franchise; can bundle w/ Prime; MGM studio muscle. (Kitty — for now.)
- **Apple TV+** — premium/prestige, deep pockets, very selective and slower; sports pedigree
  (MLB, Messi/MLS); pays well but picky; wants prestige, not volume. (Sunjay's connection.)
- **Paramount / Skydance** — post-merger, restructuring, has **CBS Sports** + Paramount+; hungry
  for differentiated content; less deep-pocketed than the big three but linear+streaming+sports
  reach. (Kitty may land here.)
- **Leverage:** having Netflix + Amazon + Apple + Paramount all live = real competitive tension,
  which is *the* mechanism for retaining IP and improving terms. Run them as **one coordinated,
  competitive process** — not four leaks. Amazon (NBA) + the live-games angle may be the wedge
  that creates the most heat.
