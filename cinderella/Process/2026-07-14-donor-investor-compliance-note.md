# Compliance Note — Can a prior MBB donor invest in the SPV?
_Session: 2026-07-14 · companion to 2026-06-24-ncaa-compliance-donor-collective-analysis.md_
_Not legal advice — Loeb + collegiate-sports counsel (risk register #12) to bless the specific structure._

**Question:** Would it be OK for a donor of a school's men's basketball program
(e.g., a Saint Joseph's MBB donor) to be an investor in that school's SPV?

**Short answer: generally yes — and it's the purest expression of the
donor-conversion thesis** (offer would-be donors a return instead of a receipt).
Their existing MBB donor base is arguably the **best first investor list**.

## Why it's generally OK
- **Booster status doesn't prohibit investing.** Donating makes someone a
  "representative of athletics interests" (booster) permanently, but that
  restricts **recruiting contact and impermissible benefits to athletes/recruits**
  — not making a for-profit investment.
- **Boosters may already fund NIL** (that's what collectives are). Booster money
  reaching genuine, FMV NIL deals for *enrolled* athletes is well-trodden ground;
  the equity return doesn't change that — it's about the *nature of the payments
  to athletes*, not who invested.

## Guardrails (the real conditions)
1. **Mixed capital — don't let the SPV be funded *exclusively* by one school's
   boosters.** All-donor funding looks like a disguised booster collective (the
   characterization risk in the 6-24 memo). One donor among a broad pool is safe.
2. **Wall the investor off from recruiting.** No contact with prospects; NIL
   never dangled as a pre-enrollment inducement / pay-for-play.
3. **Genuine, FMV-clean NIL.** Post-House, third-party deals >$600 get FMV review
   (NIL Go / College Sports Commission). The docuseries + content engine
   conveniently *creates* real NIL work to document.
4. **Securities basics.** SPV stake is a security — investor should be
   **accredited**, with Reg D disclosure. Donor history is irrelevant to securities law.
5. **Loop in school compliance / GC.** Transparent + blessed beats quiet.

## The clean framing
Two *separate* things: **a bona-fide for-profit investment** in a commercial
media/sports venture, and **separate, documented, FMV-cleared NIL** deals for
athletes — not "a booster paying players for a return."
