# Merch & Licensing — How It Actually Works + Premium-Collab Structures
_Session: 2026-07-20 · companion to 2026-07-20-revenue-vectors-downside-hedges.md (vector #2)_

Clarifies the D2C merch arm: it is **licensing through an agent**, not "pay the
school and print anything." But MC's leverage (it's the transformation, not a
random vendor) lets it control the ecosystem for its team.

---

## How college licensing actually works
- Marks are managed by a **licensing agent** — **CLC (now under Learfield)** or
  **Fanatics** (consolidating college licensing) — not the school directly.
- To put a logo on goods you become an **official licensee** through the agent:
  - **Royalty**, not a flat fee: **~8–15% of wholesale** (sometimes retail), often
    with a minimum annual guarantee.
  - **Product approval is real** — every SKU/design approved by the agent (and
    sometimes the school). No unilateral printing.
  - **Category exclusivities exist** — a category may be locked; the school's
    **Nike/adidas/UA** apparel deal can claim broad apparel rights.
- So within *your own* licensed line you control pricing, D2C channel, and margin —
  but you're one licensee inside the school's ecosystem, paying royalties + getting
  approvals. You do **not** automatically control all the school's merch.

## MC's leverage (how to control the ecosystem anyway)
1. **Fold designated licensing / merch rights into the SPV agreement** — MC gets the
   designated commercial-merch rights (or a master/co-license) **for the deal term**,
   so the team's merch routes through MC (subject to agent approvals + royalty).
2. **Or originate premium collabs and keep the majority** — same logic as MC-originated
   MMR sponsorship: MC sources/structures, keeps an **originator ~80–85%**, school
   gets its royalty, agent processes.

## Premium-brand collabs (Peter Millar, etc.) — a real, established category
Officially licensed premium/lifestyle collegiate lines already exist:
- **Peter Millar Collegiate** (golf shirts, quarter-zips, outerwear).
- **Vineyard Vines, johnnie-O, Southern Tide, B. Draddy, Rhoback** — licensed lifestyle/performance.
- **Mitchell & Ness, '47 Brand, Homefield Apparel** (Homefield = a strong D2C vintage-college brand).
- High-fashion capsules (Gucci/Balenciaga/Rowing Blazers) have done university-logo pieces.

**Three ways to structure a premium collab:**
- **Brand licenses + produces + sells; MC brokers** → MC keeps an originator cut, school royalty. Lowest lift.
- **MC licenses + commissions the brand to manufacture** → MC sells D2C, keeps the margin, pays brand a production fee/rev-share + school royalty. Most control/margin.
- **Three-way capsule** ("Making Cinderella × School × Peter Millar"), a limited drop tied to the show/season. Best story + marketing amplification.

Feel: a **$110 PM quarter-zip** with school marks is premium; even after the brand's
cut + school royalty, MC (as originator/licensee) keeps a healthy margin — and it
**elevates the whole line** above fan tees.

## D2C mechanics (operating the arm)
- **Storefront:** Shopify backbone.
- **Fulfillment:** hold inventory (higher margin, inventory risk) · **print-on-demand**
  (Printful/Printify — ~20–35% margin, zero downside) · or a **merch partner**
  (Fanjoy / Represent / Bravado) that runs the store end-to-end for a rev-share.
- **Drops:** limited runs timed to episodes airing / big games → urgency, scarcity, margin, social amplification. Player "moment" drops with NIL (same-day tee after a buzzer-beater).
- **Own the data:** the buyer list is an asset — remarket, upsell, build LTV.
- **Unit economics vs licensing:** $35 hoodie, COGS ~$12–15 → D2C keeps ~$20 (55–60%)
  vs ~$3–5 royalty under pure licensing = **4–6× more per unit**, plus the customer.

## Watch-outs before promising it
1. **Check the school's Nike/adidas/UA apparel scope** — sideline/on-court is theirs;
   lifestyle/golf (PM's lane) is usually open, but confirm no broad apparel exclusivity.
2. **The agent (Learfield/CLC or Fanatics) is in the middle** — secure merch/licensing
   rights or an originator carve-out **in the LOI up front**, not after.
3. **Approvals are real** — build design sign-off lead time into the drop calendar.

## Net
Route merch through **designated licensing rights secured in the SPV deal** (not an
assumed open field). Premium collabs (Peter Millar-type) are the high-margin,
brand-elevating SKUs that make this more than fan T-shirts. Franchise-branded +
story-driven merch sells on **attention, not wins** → the hedge property holds.
