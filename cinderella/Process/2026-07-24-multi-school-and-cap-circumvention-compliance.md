# Multi-School Rosters & Cap-Circumvention — Compliance Strategy
_Session 2026-07-24 · companion to `2026-06-24-ncaa-compliance-donor-collective-analysis.md`.
NOT legal advice — strategic scaffolding for Loeb (Brian Socolow). The CSC is
actively litigating these exact boundaries in 2026._

## Q1 — Is there a conflict / will the NCAA care if MC helps form rosters at multiple schools?

The NCAA doesn't police "conflict of interest" as such. It polices three things:

1. **Institutional control.** "Telling coaches who we'll pay and how much" reads as an
   outside entity controlling the roster = loss of institutional control. **Reframe in
   substance:** MC sets an NIL *budget* and makes deals *available*; the coach decides
   who to recruit/play; the player chooses to sign. Fund *opportunity*, not *roster
   spots*. The coach must be able to say no and mean it.
2. **Tampering / pre-enrollment inducement.** MC may only deal with players **already
   enrolled or committed** at the contracted school. Any contact/steering *before*
   commitment = impermissible recruiting inducement (brightest line).
3. **Multi-school amplifier — "shadow league steering talent."** One entity funding
   rosters at several schools it profits from looks like a cartel controlling
   competition. Danger = **cross-school player movement**. Rule: independence walls
   between SPVs; **MC never steers a player between its own schools**; each SPV arm's-length.

**The real conflict is fiduciary, not NCAA:** different investors in SPV-A vs SPV-B;
steering the best player to B harms A's investors. Needs an allocation / ethics-wall
policy at the fund level.

## Q2 — Will the NCAA claim we circumvent the rev-share cap (fund roster, then backfill via school rev-share)?

Yes — this is the model's biggest regulatory risk. The settlement's **"associated
entity" + "valid business purpose / reasonable range"** test exists specifically to
stop above-cap money routed to players through affiliated entities.

**The trap:** if MC fronts the roster and the school **"backfills" MC via rev-share
calibrated/traceable to roster spend**, the CSC calls it the school funding player comp
above its cap through a pass-through. "Backfill" itself is the red-flag word.

**The pivot — two streams that must NEVER touch:**

| Stream | What | Sized by | Funded from |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: MC → players | 3rd-party NIL for real services (docuseries, sponsor, merch, likeness) | FMV of the work | production + sponsor + MC capital |
| B: school → MC | commercial return for operating the school's commercial engine | **commercial revenue** (80% above baseline) | school's commercial upside |

Claim dies **only if all four hold:**
1. Streams not contingent on each other.
2. Stream B sized by **revenue MC generated**, never by **what MC paid players**.
3. MC never invoices the school for roster; never "reimbursement"/"backfill."
4. Timing: roster paid up front from production/sponsor/capital; rev-share arrives
   later and is demonstrably not in the roster.

**Norman's two fixes — both correct and the strongest part:**
- **Fund other schools with it** → cross-portfolio fungibility breaks traceability; no
  single school's rev-share maps to its own roster.
- **Rev-share as profit that funds next year's school** → temporal + entity separation;
  rev-share is enterprise profit to investors / next cohort.

**Unifying principle:** money is fungible at the **enterprise** level; no dollar of a
school's rev-share is earmarked to its own players. MC is a real multi-school business
redeploying commercial returns — a different fact pattern than a single-school collective.

**Non-negotiable:** MC must **not be an "associated entity"** of any school (not
school-controlled, not a booster vehicle) — an independent commercial enterprise with
its own business (franchise + documentary). ⚠️ The CSC has been aggressively classifying
even MMR companies as associated entities and is **in active litigation over overreach** —
document MC's independence + commercial substance now; price in a possible challenge
regardless of how clean the structure is.

**One-line clean structure:** _A third party pays players FMV for genuine NIL work;
separately, a commercial partner earns a revenue-benchmarked return for building the
school's commercial value; that return is enterprise profit redeployed across a
portfolio — never calibrated, never reimbursed, never earmarked._

## Q3 — Norman's on-screen role (MGM/Amazon wants him pricing players)

- On-screen **evaluator/storyteller/mentor** = great TV + compliant. Depicting him
  **"pricing player values"** or **"directing the staff"** = films the institutional-control
  + pay-for-play violations (permanent, discoverable evidence). "Pricing" also guts the
  NIL-FMV defense (NIL value ≠ basketball value → looks like a salary).
- Fix: reframe to talent/NBA-development/futures; never depict comp mechanism or roster
  direction; MC reserves depiction approval; compliance reads the cut.

## Q4 — Can Norman advise/consult the coaching staff?

- Advice ≠ control. Outside experts advise programs routinely. Danger = **concentration**
  (funder + pricer + director in one head) and the **recruiting** line.
- Fixes: (1) separate roles inside MC (advice vs money), or (2) school-employee for the
  basketball function w/ MC money run arm's-length — **but see Q5, ownership breaks #2**.
- Litmus: can the coach reject his advice with **zero** funding consequence? Must be yes.

## Q5 — Ownership + dual employment

- **Owning MC poisons the school-employee path.** As owner he'd sit on **both sides** of the
  MC↔school deal — conflicted insider, worse circumvention optic, recusal guts the role.
  → If he owns MC, **don't take a school-employee seat at an MC school.**
- **Employed by two MC schools at once = worst case:** divided loyalty between competitors
  (BC & Wake both ACC), breaks anti-steering wall, no AD accepts it, torches MC independence.
  Title (consultant vs GM) irrelevant.
- **Resolution:** Norman stays **external at MC/parent level** — franchise basketball
  architect, not any school's employee. Ownership is the reason to stay OUT, not in.

## Q6 — Mentorship of players (allowable, bounded)

- **Yes**, and it's the cleanest, highest-value role. Enrolled players: game, mindset,
  NBA/draft development, performance, brand — all fine as a **sanctioned, disclosed**
  advisor providing **expertise/time, not benefits**.
- Not: contacting **prospects** (booster rule), acting as their **agent**, tying
  marketability to specific comp/inducement, or being the **checkbook**.
- Aligns compliance lane + story lane: "NBA lifer mentors underdog kids toward the pros."
