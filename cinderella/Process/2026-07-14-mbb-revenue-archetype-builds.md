## Synthesis — the lesson for Making Cinderella
- All three land at a **~$8–10M commercial (donor-excluded) base** and **~$11–16M
  with donor.** That's the realistic *matured* ceiling an 80/20 rev-share applies to.
- **Relevance + right-sized-and-filled capacity beats raw market/arena size.**
  Dayton (packed small arena) and Gonzaga (national brand, tiny arena) are the
  profitable models; **Memphis proves a big arena is a liability if you can't fill
  it** — even a Top-25 team didn't fill 18,000 seats, and margins went negative.
- **This sharpens the MC thesis:** the variable that matters is **national
  relevance**, not the building — exactly what celebrity + Netflix + a top-10
  roster manufactures. A nationally-relevant Cinderella school looks like
  **Gonzaga's shape** (light gate, heavy national licensing/media/sponsorship/units)
  — **plus** the brand-new SPV streams (doc, streaming, national sponsor, content)
  that none of these three have today.
- **Model implication:** apply the rev-share to a *capacity-aware* organic PCR
  (~$8–10M mature ceiling, less in Year 1), book the big surplus in **separate new
  SPV streams**, and treat donor per the revised policy (conversion + incremental 50/50).
