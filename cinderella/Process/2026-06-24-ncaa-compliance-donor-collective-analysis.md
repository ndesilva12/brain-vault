# NCAA Compliance & Donor Collective Analysis
_Session: 2026-06-24_

---

## The Core Question

Can a school donor or benefactor invest in their school's Making Cinderella SPV (and not other school SPVs) without creating an NCAA compliance problem?

---

## NCAA Framework: Third Party vs. Donor Collective

The NCAA has created a de facto two-tier "third party" system:

| | True Third Party | Donor Collective |
|---|---|---|
| Can pay athletes NIL | Yes | Yes |
| Can recruit with NIL promise | Yes (arms-length) | No (inducement rule) |
| Counts against school rev-share cap | No | No |
| Exposed to school sanctions | No | Yes — school is liable |
| FMV requirement scrutinized | Loosely | Same, but more suspect |
| Multi-school possible | Yes | Structurally no |

**Key insight:** Collectives are third parties in legal structure but treated as institutional affiliates in substance. They get the label without the full protection.

---

## What Donor Collectives CAN and CAN'T Do

**Can:**
- Pay athletes NIL for legitimate name/image/likeness use
- Pay unlimited amounts — NO cap (explicit circumvention of the House settlement's $20M school rev-share limit)
- Fund docuseries, podcasts, social content, ambassador roles

**Can't (theoretically, rarely enforced):**
- Promise NIL to recruits before enrollment to induce transfer/signing
- Coordinate directly with coaching staff on NIL tied to enrollment
- Pay athletes as a condition of staying at the school

**The crackdown coming:** House settlement created NIL clearinghouses at conference level to review fair market value. If a collective pays a backup player $300K to post 4 Instagram stories, a clearinghouse flags it. Not fully implemented yet.

---

## The Honest Compliance Answer

**Nothing stops a donor collective from doing exactly what Making Cinderella does — including a single-school version — from a strict NCAA compliance standpoint.**

If a collective creates a real docuseries about their program and pays athletes NIL for documented on-camera work, the NCAA has almost no enforceable rule to stop it. As long as:
- Athletes actually appear on camera (real NIL use)
- Payment isn't contingent on athletic performance
- It isn't dangled in front of recruits before enrollment

This actually cuts IN FAVOR of Making Cinderella's compliance posture. If even the most restricted entity in the ecosystem (a single-school booster collective) can do a version of this, a multi-school PE-backed commercial franchise is well within bounds.

---

## The Donor-Exclusive SPV Risk (Still Real)

Even though compliance is broadly sound, school-specific SPVs funded **exclusively** by that school's donors carry a characterization risk. The NCAA could treat it as a booster collective regardless of the parent franchise's multi-school structure.

**Fix:** Don't make donors the exclusive capital source for school SPVs. Mix in institutional/PE capital. Or push donors to invest at the parent level with economic allocation to their school's performance.

But this is a structural mitigation, not a fundamental compliance problem.

---

## The Real Moat: Narrative + Capital Structure

The compliance analysis eventually leads here — the actual barrier to entry is not legal, it's structural and narrative:

### 1. The Narrative Lock

The Cinderella story is **structurally unavailable to big programs.** You cannot manufacture underdog authenticity.

- Kentucky producing a docuseries = another Hard Knocks. No arc, no stakes, no miracle.
- The story only works at suffering mid-majors. The product itself only exists there.
- Any collective attempting this at a blue blood makes a different, lesser product nobody's asking for.

### 2. Mid-Major Collectives Don't Exist or Can't Capitalize

The schools with the right story (suffering mid-majors) don't have organized collectives with real capital. The schools with organized collectives (blue bloods) don't have the right story.

### 3. Making Cinderella Creates the Capital Vehicle Mid-Majors Never Had

Instead of competing with mid-major donor communities, Making Cinderella **brings them to life** as investors:

- Traditional collective: donor puts in $50K → disappears into player salaries → zero return
- Making Cinderella SPV: donor puts in $50K → equity in a national docuseries + championship roster → real commercial return

The money does MORE. The donor gets MORE. The school gets MORE. This isn't a threat to existing collectives — it's a category upgrade that invites **more capital than mid-major donors would otherwise commit**, because there's now a return on the investment.

### 4. No Collective Can Go Multi-School

Multi-school is antithetical to a collective's identity and purpose. Alabama boosters won't fund Georgia's NIL budget. The franchise structure cannot be replicated by a collective without ceasing to be a collective.

---

## Strategic Implication

**Don't frame compliance as the primary moat in investor or school conversations.** The compliance story is solid — but it's table stakes, not differentiation.

The real pitch:
- Only mid-majors have the story. Only Making Cinderella has the infrastructure to tell it at scale.
- We're not competing with donor collectives at those schools — we're creating the investment vehicle they never had.
- Speed of execution and signed LOIs (Steph + Davidson) are the actual defensibility.

Lock up schools fast. The celebrity + LOI combination is the moat.
