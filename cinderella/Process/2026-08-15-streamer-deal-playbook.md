# Streamer Deal Playbook — IP, Deal Types, Viewership & Buyer-by-Buyer
_Strategy reference · 2026-08-15 · internal · NOT legal advice — confirm any deal specifics with entertainment counsel (Loeb / Brian Socolow)._
_Companion to: `cinderella/CLAUDE.md` (IP non-negotiables), `Process/2026-08-11-commercial-vs-revenue-rights-and-donor-path-memo.md`._

> **The one line that governs everything below:** *whoever brings the money owns the
> franchise.* If the streamer finances the show, they own it. If **we** finance it (PE / SPV),
> we own it and license them the right to distribute. Keeping the streamer a **licensee, not a
> financier**, is the whole game.

---

## 1. "IP" is not one thing — disaggregate it

A show bundles several **separable** rights. "Who owns the show" ≠ "who owns the franchise" ≠
"who distributes it." Any of these can be split, shared, time-limited, or carved out:

1. **Format / franchise rights** — the *Making Cinderella* concept; the right to make more
   seasons, new schools, spinoffs, international versions. **The crown jewel.**
2. **Copyright in the finished episodes** — the actual filmed content.
3. **Distribution / exhibition rights** — the license to stream it: territory, window, term,
   exclusivity.
4. **Ancillary / derivative** — merch, music, live events, games, remakes.
5. **Trademark / brand** — the name and logo.

We can own #1, #2, #4, #5 and *license* #3 for a term. That is the Wrexham posture and our target.

## 2. The ownership spectrum (it is NOT binary)

From most-streamer-control to most-MC-control:

| Structure | Who finances | Who owns IP | Our position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Full buyout / work-for-hire** | Streamer 100% (cost-plus, ~10–30% fee) | Streamer owns *everything*, in perpetuity | Producer-for-hire. Fees only. **No franchise.** ❌ |
| **B. Cost-plus w/ carve-outs** | Streamer 100% | Streamer owns show; we retain *specific* rights (format, merch, backend) | Better; needs leverage ➖ |
| **C. Co-production / co-finance** | Both | Shared — split by territory or by right | Genuine partnership ✅ |
| **D. License (we own, they rent)** | **Us (SPV/PE)** | **We keep copyright + format + all IP**; grant a bounded exhibition license | Rights revert on expiry. **TARGET** ✅ |
| **E. Finished-title acquisition** | Us, fully | We own everything; sell a license after it's made | Max ownership, max risk on us ➖ |

**Even "we own it" has strings.** In a license deal (D) they still contract for **exclusivity**,
**holdbacks** (no competing version during the term), **options** on future seasons,
**first-look / last-refusal** on spinoffs, and **approval rights**. "We own it" realistically means
*"we own it, subject to the distributor's negotiated restrictions for the license term, after
which rights revert cleanly to us."* Still miles better than a buyout — it's an asset we keep and
re-license.

## 3. The deal-type taxonomy (plain English)

### A. How a single show gets bought / owned
| Deal | Plain English | MC fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost-plus buyout** (commissioned original) | They pay cost + margin; own the show & all rights forever. | ❌ Avoid — loses the franchise |
| **License** (we own, they rent) | We finance & own it; they pay to stream it for a set term/territory; rights revert to us. | ✅ Target |
| **Co-production / co-finance** | Both fund it; both share ownership & rights (by territory or by right). | ✅ Good |
| **Pre-sale / pre-license** | They pre-pay to license *before* it's made; we use that cash to fund it and keep ownership. | ✅ Strong |
| **Acquisition (finished title)** | We build it ourselves, then sell them a license after. | ➖ Only if fully self-funded |

### B. Pre-greenlight / development deals
| Deal | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Option / development deal** | They pay a smaller sum to develop (sizzle/script) and hold an exclusive right to greenlight later. |
| **Pilot / proof-of-concept order** | They fund one episode/sizzle to decide on the series. |
| **Script/format-to-series ("if/come")** | Conditional greenlight — triggers when we hit a condition (lock a name, deliver a sizzle). |
| **Straight-to-series order** | Full season(s) committed upfront, no pilot. |

### C. Producer-relationship deals (company-level)
| Deal | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **First-look** | We show them each new project first; they get first refusal, we can shop elsewhere if they pass. |
| **Overall** | Annual retainer to develop exclusively for them for a term. (Watch: can capture our IP.) |
| **Output** | They take a producer's entire slate over a period, sight unseen. |

### D. Economic terms layered on top of any deal
| Term | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Backend / profit participation** | We keep a share of ongoing profit/revenue. Rare with streamers — worth fighting for. |
| **Performance bonuses / viewership bumps** | Extra pay if it hits renewal/viewership milestones. **Where viewership becomes money.** |
| **Windowing** | Rights split across time/exclusivity windows (festival → SVOD → ad-tier/FAST). |
| **Holdbacks / exclusivity** | Limits on where else it runs during the term — even when we own it. |

## 4. How important are viewership numbers to us?

**Viewership is not our paycheck — it's our flywheel.** In a flat license/buyout, our season check
is fixed at signing, regardless of audience. But almost everything that turns MC into a *franchise*
is viewership-driven:

| Does viewership move this? | Answer |
|---|---|
| Season-1 license check (flat-fee) | **No** — locked at signing |
| **Renewal / next season & school** | **Yes — existential** to the multi-season thesis |
| **Our price + IP leverage next deal** | **Yes** — a proven hit lets us charge more and keep more IP |
| **Sponsorship / jersey-patch revenue** | **Yes** — eyeball-priced SPV revenue |
| **Attracting next celebrity + school** | **Yes** — talent/programs chase a winner |
| **School success-fee (Leg B)** | **Yes** — popularity drives the school's commercial revenue our fee tracks |
| **Performance bonuses** (if negotiated) | **Yes — directly** |

Two rules:
- **Track the buyer's metric, not raw viewership.** Netflix = completion/hours; Apple = prestige /
  subscriber halo (a "small" Apple hit can be a big win); ESPN = ratings + ad value. Renewal is
  decided on *their* scoreboard, often a non-public number.
- **If we believe it travels, structure for upside** (backend, viewership bonuses, retained
  ancillary), not just a flat fee. A buyout insulates us from a flop but **caps us on a hit.**

## 5. Will a streamer help procure the celebrity?

**Capability: very high. Willingness: real but conditional — and double-edged.**
- A platform's imprimatur de-risks the project for the talent; agents move fast for "Apple wants to
  make this with you." Overall/first-look talent is the strongest form of help.
- **But every star they procure, they'll claim they "created"** — eroding our fees and, worse, our
  IP position. Talent attached *through* the streamer is loyal to the streamer.
- It inverts leverage: if they bring the star, the money, and distribution, we brought only the
  format + schools.
- **Mitigant (already in motion):** we self-attached **Curry (Unanimous)**. Keep bringing our own
  anchor(s). Ideal split: *we* bring one anchor + format + schools; *they* bring distribution +
  money + supplemental talent. If they insist on procuring the lead, **price it in fees, not
  ownership.**

## 6. Pre-product, pre-celebrity involvement — how common?

**Common as a development/option deal; uncommon as a full cold greenlight.** The ladder before
anything is shot:
1. **Option / development deal** (most common) — fund a sizzle, hold exclusivity, option to order.
2. **First-look / overall** with us as producer.
3. **If/come** — "greenlight when you lock [name] + deliver a sizzle."
4. **Straight-to-series with nothing shot & no talent** — *rare*; reserved for trusted producers or
   a **proprietary format a rival could steal**, where they **pre-empt to lock exclusivity.**

What flips it to #4:
- **Defensibility** — if we control the schools, access, and format, they pre-empt rather than let a
  competitor get it. That's our premise.
- **De-risking** — even when they love it they usually want *one* of: an anchor name, a sizzle, or a
  trusted production partner. **We're past "no attachment" — the Curry/Davidson LOI is our anchor.**

**Double-edged (again):** early streamer money = they own more. Purest IP path is to fund
development ourselves (PE/SPV) to a sizzle + locked anchor, then bring them in as a **distributor.**
Competitive tension across buyers is what turns "development deal" into "preemptive order on our
terms." Keep every conversation live and make sure they know it.

## 7. Buyer-by-buyer (assuming each wants it)

Ratings are relative. **Q1** willing to procure talent · **Q2** capable of good talent · **Q3** how
hard on IP · **Q4** IP leverage · **Q5** common buy structure · **Q6** most likely MC outcome.

| Buyer | Q1 Procure (willing) | Q2 Procure (capable) | Q3 IP push | Q4 IP leverage | Q5 Common structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HBO / Max** (WBD) | High | Very High (prestige) | Hard (docs softer) | High prestige — but WBD cash-stressed = a crack | Owned originals; docs often co-pro/license |
| **Apple TV+** | Very High | Very High (A-list film) | **Hardest** | Very High (infinite balance sheet) | Full buyout, premium fees, perpetual ownership |
| **Netflix** | Very High | Very High (global) | Hard | Very High (scale/reach) | Owned global originals; sports docs via producer partners (Drive to Survive) |
| **Amazon** (Prime/MGM) | High | High (rising) | Medium-Hard | High (+ live NBA from '25-26) | Owned but flexible; co-pro/first-look common |

| **Skydance Sports** (producer, not a platform) | High | High (proven sports-doc shop) | Medium-Hard (wants format equity/backend) | Medium (production muscle + Paramount path) | Co-production/co-finance, then P+ or license out |
| **Disney** (Hulu / ESPN) | Medium-High | High (**ESPN unmatched for sports**) | **Hardest tier** | Very High (**30 for 30** brand + owns college-hoops ecosystem) | Owned originals; ESPN Films co-pro/acquires docs |

### Q6 — most likely outcome with MC
- **HBO/Max:** Prestige, awards-oriented, purest "obscure-school Cinderella." Wants to own, but
  WBD's cash squeeze + doc-unit norms give a real license/co-pro shot if we bring PE. Smallest check,
  biggest prestige. (Max also streams March Madness via Turner — same cross-promo lever as Paramount.)
- **Apple:** Richest deal + best talent help (Sudeikis/Ted Lasso-adjacent), **hardest on IP.** Likely
  producer-for-hire with strong fees unless we self-finance and force a license. Best money, worst
  franchise terms.
- **Netflix:** Fastest, most global, biggest audience. Defaults to ownership — but the **Drive to
  Survive / Box to Box** precedent is our template to keep producer economics if we bring format +
  money. Middle on IP.
- **Amazon:** Best *strategic synergy* — launches **live NBA in '25-26**, a built-in hoops audience +
  promo engine. Owns-but-flexible; likely co-pro. Good money, decent IP, great fit.
- **Paramount:** Best cross-promotion for a *college* property — **CBS broadcasts March Madness.**
  Most IP-flexible / deal-hungry, smallest checks, least prestige-talent pull. Sleeper strategic fit.
- **Skydance Sports:** Not a buyer — a **co-production ally** that packages & sells (likely into
  Paramount+, unlocking March Madness). Powerful partner *or* IP threat depending on terms. **Treat
  like EverWonder: service vendor, no backend, no format equity.**
- **Disney/ESPN:** **Most credible sports-doc home** (30 for 30) and **best in-season promo** — ESPN
  airs college hoops Nov→March, a bigger engine than any three-week tournament window. But as
  ownership-hungry as Apple. Best credibility + promo, tied-hardest franchise fight.

**One-line read:** Apple = money · Netflix = reach · HBO = prestige · **Disney/ESPN, Amazon &
Paramount = the three with real basketball distribution muscle** (ESPN's is strongest, all-season) ·
Skydance = a producer to use carefully, not a buyer.

### Skydance ≠ Paramount (footnote for the meeting)
Skydance is a **production + financing company**, and since the **Aug 2025 merger it now owns
Paramount** (David Ellison, Chairman/CEO of Paramount Skydance). A meeting labeled "Skydance" is
almost certainly **Skydance Sports** — a producer/packager that would co-produce and shop MC (likely
to Paramount+), **not a platform buying a license.** Different IP dynamic: a co-pro partner will
angle for format equity/backend — the capture risk our model already guards against.

## 8. MC redlines (bring to every buyer conversation)

**Never give up:**
- **Format / franchise rights** (more seasons, new schools, spinoffs, international). Non-negotiable.
- **The brand / trademark** (*Making Cinderella*).
- **The IP itself whenever we self-finance.** Our PE/SPV capital is the *mechanism* that lets us own.
- **Ancillary / merch / live** where at all possible.

**Willing to grant:**
- An **exclusive distribution license** — bounded **term**, **territory**, and **window**, with clean
  **reversion** on expiry.
- Reasonable **holdbacks** during the term and **options / first-look** on future seasons (priced).

**Traps to refuse:**
- Letting the streamer **finance production** — their money buys our franchise. (This is *the* trap a
  buyer who loves it will spring: "we'll just fund it.")
- **Cost-plus buyouts** and **overall deals** that capture the producer/IP.
- Pricing streamer-procured talent in **ownership** instead of **fees**.

**Our leverage (name it, use it):**
1. **PE / SPV self-financing** (own because we pay).
2. **Self-attached anchor talent** (Curry) + our own celebrity pipeline.
3. **Proprietary, defensible format** — we control the schools, access, and story no blue-blood can
   replicate.
4. **Competitive tension** — multiple buyers live at once turns a development deal into a preemptive
   order on our terms.
