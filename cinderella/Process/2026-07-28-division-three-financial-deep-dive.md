# D3 Route — Financial Deep Dive (Johns Hopkins example)
_Session: 2026-07-28 · full 5/6-season arc economics, roster legality, revenue build, and the
VC ask. Companion spreadsheet: `Cinderella Corp Model - D3 (Johns Hopkins).xlsx` (new `SPV_D3`

editable input in the sheet._

## 1. Timeline — the one hard correction
- **D3→D1 reclassification is a 4-year transition, tournament-ineligible the entire time**, and
  a reclassifying school **cannot take the conference auto-bid** during it (win the league
  tourney and the bid passes to the runner-up).
- So with a **D3 season in Year 1**:
  - **Y1 (2027-28): D3** — apply Feb 2027 (moratorium lifts); win the D3 national title. ✅
  - **Y2-Y5 (2028-32): the four D1 transition years — all ineligible.**
  - **Y6 (2032-33): first Dance eligibility.**
- **Net: it's a 6-season arc to the tournament, not 5.** "Compete in D1 the following year" — yes,
  they join a D1 league in Y2; but Year 5 is the *last ineligible* transition year, and the
  Dance is Year 6. I built the model on 6 seasons, keeping your exact Y1-Y5 NIL plan and adding
  a Y6 tournament year at a sustained $14M.
- _If you insist on a 5-year window ending in the Dance, the only way is to skip the standalone
  D3 season and start the D1 clock in Y1 — which throws away the D3-championship hook. Not worth
  it; the 6-year build is the better story anyway._

## 2. Roster legality — can a signing bonus legally cover tuition?
**Yes, with guardrails.** D3 bars athletic *scholarships*, but **NIL is permitted across all
divisions.** A third party (collective/company) can pay a player NIL of any size; the player
uses it to pay tuition. Three compliance lines:
1. **Genuine NIL, not a disguised scholarship** — the payment must be for real name/image/
   likeness activity. Being cast in a Netflix series *is* legitimate, valuable NIL work → this
   is the strongest "valid business purpose" defense available (directly answers the July-2025
   College Sports Commission scrutiny).
2. **No pre-enrollment inducement** — you recruit on the platform/opportunity and paper the NIL
   *after* enrollment, not as a promise to a recruit/portal player.
3. **Not funneled through the school.**
- At Hopkins (~$90K all-in COA), a ~$90K+ bonus makes a player whole; ~13 players ≈ $1.2M just
  for attendance — which is why **$1M in Y1 is tight but workable for a strong-but-not-loaded
  D3 roster.**
- **Your fallback is real and powerful:** D1/D2 transfers are immediately eligible at D3, and at
  an elite academic like Hopkins you can attract high-major transfers motivated by *the Netflix
  platform + a Hopkins degree*, playing partly for exposure rather than cash — cutting NIL cost.
  **A $1M D3 roster of motivated D1/D2 transfers wins a D3 national title comfortably.** Loeb
  papers all of it.

## 5. The thing that actually matters: it can self-fund
Because **platform licensing + celebrity-driven sponsorship + merch are large and scale with the
content — not the on-court tier** — total revenue exceeds NIL+costs in *every* season of the base
case. **Cumulative cash flow never goes negative → peak external funding need ≈ $0.** Even a
**conservative case** (platform at $0.6M/hr instead of $1M, sponsorship/merch roughly halved)
only dips ~$1-2M underwater at the trough before the back half recovers.

**So the entire risk collapses to one question:** *will a streamer pay a premium license, and will
an anchor sponsor commit, on the strength of the celebrity?* If yes, the roster cost is largely
paid by the content and the thing self-funds. If the platform balks, you're funding NIL out of
pocket and the exposure is the gross **$53M**.

## 6. "How big a check do you need?" — the VC answer
> **"$20M, committed and drawn in milestone tranches — but understand what you're really
> underwriting."**

- **$20M** fully covers the S1-S4 NIL ramp plus a buffer even if early-year revenue disappoints,
  and it's a fraction of a *single* D1 SPV ($10-12M in one year). In the base case most of it is
  never drawn or is returned quickly.
- **What the VC is actually underwriting is not a basketball roster — it's the celebrity +
  platform license + anchor sponsor.** Those three de-risk the whole model; the roster is the
  cheap part. Frame the raise that way.
- **Equity discipline (the sharp part):** the model's 30% "strategic partner" slice is worth
  ~$24M of net CF. But on a *fully-drawn* $20M that's only **~1.2x over 6 years** — a bad VC deal
  — while in the self-funding base case (they deploy ≈$0) that same 30% is a giveaway. Either way
  a flat 30% is mispriced. **Price the equity to *actual* deployment:** a committed facility with
  a draw fee + a smaller equity kicker that steps up only if they truly fund the roster. And note
  a VC may not even be the right capital here — a sponsor advance or the celebrity could cover the
  timing gap more cheaply.

## 7. What to consider (risks & watch-items)
- **Platform-rate credibility.** $1M/hr for a D3 unknown in S1 is the aggressive assumption a VC
  will challenge. Defense: this isn't "a D3 team," it's "[megastar]'s Netflix passion project."
  Keep a conservative-rate sensitivity ready.
- **The move is NCAA- and will-gated.** 4-year calendar you can't buy down; moratorium to Feb
  2027; Hopkins must actually *choose* to go D1 (its existing D1 lacrosse + Bloomberg make it the
  most plausible elite academic, but it's still a board decision).
- **Ineligible-years narrative.** Five straight seasons with no Dance — you're selling "the
  climb." Winning the D3 title (S1) and the conference (even without the auto-bid) are the
  interim climaxes; the story has to carry ~$40M of spend on buildup alone.
- **Don't fold NIL into production.** Keep it in the SPV (IP/backend firewall vs. EverWonder).
- **Compliance papering** (NIL vs. scholarship vs. inducement) is load-bearing — Loeb up front.
- **Revenue timing vs. NIL timing** — NIL is paid before/during the season; license & sponsor
  cash lands on its own schedule → the committed facility is for *timing*, not just downside.
- **Unit economics of the Dance are back-loaded** — most NCAA-unit value accrues Y7-Y11, past
  this window; the tournament's near-term value is narrative/commercial, not unit cash.

## 8. Bottom line
The D3 route is not a cheaper D1 — it's a **content-first, potentially self-funding vehicle**
whose downside is floored by low roster cost and whose upside is a ~$81M 6-year net if the
celebrity-premium revenue shows up. **Ask a VC for a $20M milestone-drawn facility, priced as a
facility (not a flat 30% equity), and pitch it as underwriting the celebrity + platform deal, not
a basketball team.** Confirm the numbers in the attached `SPV_D3` tab and dial the blue inputs to
run your own cases.
