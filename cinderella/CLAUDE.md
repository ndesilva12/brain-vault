# Cinderella Project — Project Context

> Norman is **Founder and CEO of Cinderella Corp.**, the parent IP company behind _Making Cinderella_ — a sports entertainment franchise pairing high-profile celebrities with small Division I college basketball programs, structured as Wrexham-style documentary investments.

---

## The model

The format pairs celebrities with schools through **authentic connections** (alma mater, regional roots, adopted-city narratives), injects private capital to drive program transformation, and captures the season as a premium documentary series. Returns come from streaming licensing, sponsorships, NIL commissions, and media rights.

**Norman's role:** Operational lead — EVP of Basketball Operations / GM role in any deal structure. His moat is the combination of NBA scouting background (Celtics, Spurs, Rockets, 76ers), coaching experience, Babson finance training, and the franchise IP architecture he's built.

**Pitch materials:** cinderella.short.gy/deck and cinderella.short.gy/video

**Reference benchmarks:** Welcome to Wrexham (structural and commercial model), Drive to Survive, Quarterback, Full Swing, Last Chance U

---

## Structure

- **Cinderella Corp.** holds all IP and backend rights.
- **Per-season SPVs** collect distributor license fees and distribute investor returns.
- **Production partners are service vendors** — no IP equity, no backend participation. This is non-negotiable.

### NCAA structural insight

The LLC can own revenue rights (tickets, media allocations, sponsorships, tournament units) **without** owning the team itself — satisfying institutional control requirements while giving investors meaningful economic returns.

## Legal & compliance

### NCAA Compliance Posture — Summary (see Process/2026-06-24-ncaa-compliance-donor-collective-analysis.md for full analysis)

**Bottom line:** Making Cinderella's structure is NCAA-compliant. The compliance case is solid but is NOT the primary moat — don't lead with it.

**Key findings:**
- Donor collectives are technically third parties but treated as institutional affiliates in substance. They can pay unlimited NIL (no cap), but can't use NIL to induce recruits before enrollment.
- Nothing in the NCAA rulebook explicitly stops a donor collective from producing a single-school docuseries. This cuts in our favor — if even a booster collective can do it, a multi-school PE-backed franchise is well within bounds.
- Loeb & Loeb are now formally engaged (Brian Socolow, bsocolow@loeb.com; John Kulback CC). Engagement agreement sent 6/15/26.
- School-specific SPVs funded **exclusively** by that school's donors carry a characterization risk (looks like a booster collective). Fix: require mixed capital (institutional + donor) in each school SPV, or push donors to invest at the parent level.

**The real moat is narrative and structural — not legal:**
1. The Cinderella story is structurally unavailable to big programs. Blue blood collectives can't replicate it — they'd just make another Hard Knocks.
2. Mid-major schools with the right story don't have organized collectives with real capital.
3. Making Cinderella creates the investment vehicle those communities never had — converting pure donor subsidy into equity-backed commercial investment with a real return.
4. No collective can go multi-school without ceasing to be a collective.

## Key principles

- **IP ownership is non-negotiable.** Cinderella Corp. owns all IP and backend; production partners are service vendors.
- **Structural signals matter early.** Catch casual framings ("flat 10% fee") before they become assumed positions.
- **Strict pairing criteria.** Small D1 + authentic connection. Don't dilute.
- **Franchise pitch > single-show pitch.** Multiple celebrity anchors (Curry + Hart) elevate to franchise-level streaming deal.
- **Map counterparty value-capture vectors preemptively** and build contractual counters.
- **Compliance is table stakes, not the pitch.** The moat is narrative (only mid-majors have the story), capital structure (we create the investment vehicle mid-major donors never had), and execution speed (signed LOIs). Lock up schools fast.
- **We don't compete with mid-major donor communities — we create them.** Making Cinderella converts pure booster subsidy into return-generating equity investment, which invites more capital than those communities would otherwise commit.
